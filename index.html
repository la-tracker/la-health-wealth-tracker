Why this tracker exists

A person's address is one of the strongest predictors of their health and financial security. This tool puts that connection on a map, so residents, advocates, and council staff can see where the gaps are widest and act on them.

How the indices work

Every district gets three scores on a 0 to 100 scale. Financial and community resources covers income, school ratings, homeownership, and nature access. Health risk covers substance use, chronic disease, and life expectancy, and a low number is the good result. Neighborhood access covers food retail, financial services, and recreation.

Built for civic action

Built by Zoe White, a Los Angeles high school student, to get more young people into city data. District shapes are the official city boundaries from the 2012 redistricting. The 2021 boundaries are the next upgrade. Sources include LA County Public Health, USC Neighborhood Data for Social Change, USDA, CFPB, and LA City Planning. Read the methodology
